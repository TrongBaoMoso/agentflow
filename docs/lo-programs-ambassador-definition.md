# What an Ambassador is, and who can qualify

Answer to point 3 of Thuan's 21 Aug review: *"I think we give the wrong definition of an
Ambassador. Please ask AI to explain what is an ambassador and who can qualify for it."*

Points 1 and 2 of that review are already shipped — see
[the PR](https://github.com/LoanFactory-Inc/lf-homepage/pull/2291). This one is not a wording
change, so it is written down first.

---

## What the page says today

> "Know a good loan officer? Get paid to bring them here. You already know who the good ones
> are. Tell them about Loan Factory. When one of them joins, you get a referral bonus."

That is a **referral program**. Our own glossary on the same page says the bonus is "the standard
bonus Loan Factory already pays anyone here who brings in a loan officer" — unchanged by this
program, identical at every level, no application needed. So the page's headline describes
something every employee already has, and the word *Ambassador* is doing no work in it.

The Level 1 row makes the same problem visible: **"Everyone here — no budget."** A title that
everyone holds by default is not a title. Seth's program document is actually clearer than our
page on this: the only two rungs it *names* are "Loan Officer Ambassador" and "Senior Loan
Officer Ambassador" — Levels 2 and 3. Level 1 in that document is participation in the referral
program, not a rank of ambassadorship.

## What an Ambassador actually is

An ambassador **represents** — that is the whole word. Outside our walls the term means the same
thing everywhere: a person a company **selects** to stand for it in public, under a standing
agreement, and supports with money and tools so they can keep doing it. What separates it from a
referral program is not the size of the reward; it is that referral is a transaction anyone can
do once, and ambassadorship is a **role** with a name, a mandate and an obligation.

For us, in one line:

> **An Ambassador is a Loan Factory loan officer who puts their own name and their own audience
> behind Loan Factory in public — and whom Loan Factory pays to do it.**

Two halves, and both have to be true:

| | |
|---|---|
| **They represent us in public** | They post about Loan Factory to their own network, on their own accounts, under their own name — 2–3 times a week. At the senior level they host webinars and stand up at events. Their reputation is on the line, not just their contact list. |
| **We fund them** | Up to $500 a month at Level 2, up to $1,000 at Level 3, plus their loan officer data subscription, LinkedIn tools, and lists of top producers at other lenders. Company money behind a private individual's name. |

Recruiting is the **result** we hold them to, not the definition. Referring is what anyone can
do quietly; ambassadorship is being publicly identifiable as a Loan Factory person and being
paid for that visibility.

## Who can qualify

**Level 2 — Loan Officer Ambassador**

- An active, licensed Loan Officer at Loan Factory, in good standing.
- An audience worth funding: roughly 1,000+ professional and social connections.
- Willing to be publicly identified with Loan Factory, and to post 2–3 times a week — we write
  the content, they put their name on it.
- Able to explain our platform, pricing, technology and support without help. An Ambassador who
  has to phone someone before answering a question is not representing us; they are relaying.
- Approved by leadership. This is the part that makes it a title.
- Keeps it by producing: 3 loan officers recruited and onboarded per 90 days, or a pipeline
  clearly moving toward that.

**Level 3 — Senior Loan Officer Ambassador**

- Everything above, plus roughly 2,000+ connections.
- A record of having actually recruited, not just a willingness to.
- Comfortable in a conversation with a branch manager or a team leader, not only with an
  individual loan officer.
- 6+ recruited and onboarded per 90 days.

**Who does not qualify.** Anyone unlicensed — the title says Loan Officer. Team Leaders and
Corporate Coaches by virtue of their position: leading a team is not ambassadorship, and Seth's
document says so explicitly. And nobody at all by default.

## What this changes on the page

1. **The headline stops selling the referral bonus.** It is not what this page is for. It moves
   into one line of context.
2. **Level 1 stops being called a level.** It becomes a single sentence — every employee can
   refer a loan officer and earn the standard bonus, and needs neither this page nor a title to
   do it. The ladder starts at Ambassador.
3. **The page leads with the job, not the money.** What an Ambassador does in public, then what
   Loan Factory puts behind them, then what they are held to.
4. **"Marketing budget" becomes the support it is,** rather than the reward the page currently
   frames it as: three tiers of money with a bonus attached reads like a pay scale, which is why
   the levels felt like the point.

## Sources for the general meaning

  — ambassadors are hand-picked, brought on under a formal agreement, for a long-term
  promotional relationship.
- [World Employer Branding Community — advocacy vs ambassadors](https://www.worldemployerbrandingday.community/post/employee-advocacy-ambassadors-what-s-the-difference-and-why-should-you-care)
  — advocacy is content sharing at scale; ambassadorship is deeper, long-term representation.
- [Sociabble — employee ambassador programs](https://www.sociabble.com/blog/employee-advocacy/employee-ambassador-program/)
  — employees empowered and equipped to represent the brand, as a standing role.

  connection thresholds, budgets, 90-day numbers, and the "Team Leader status does not
  auto-qualify" rule all come from this document.
