### The main change

Neither page used to admit the other one existed. A loan officer landing on either one had no way to
tell whether they were on the right page — and I think that, more than any paragraph, is what made
them hard. Both pages now open with a two-card chooser: *"Pick the one that is you"*, one sentence
per program, with the page you are on marked.

### Ambassador — what changed

The old headline was **"Recruit. Earn. Get Supported."** — three verbs and no object; it never said
who the page was for or what you get. The first hard number on the page was Level 1's marketing
budget: **None**. So the page opened by telling you that you get nothing, while the thing you
actually get paid — a referral bonus on every loan officer who joins — appeared once, as a bullet,
with no explanation.

Now:

- The headline states the offer: *"Know a good loan officer? Get paid to bring them here."*
- The first sentence names the bonus and puts a number on the marketing money — up to $1,000 a month.
- One line fixes the mental model: **every loan officer who joins because of you pays you a referral
  bonus, and that is the same at every level. The levels are only about how much marketing money we
  put behind you.**
- The three level cards — about forty bullets between them — became one four-row comparison table.
  On a phone it stacks into labelled blocks instead of scrolling sideways.
- The program itself is three numbered steps: turn Recruiting on in ALLY → we post for you → send us
  names. Nothing to apply for.

### Loan Officer Recruiter — what changed

Three lines on the old page could be read backwards, and each wrong reading changes the program:

| Old line | What we meant | How it reads |
|---|---|---|
| "12+ verified units to qualify" | the person you recruit needs 12+ closed loans | **you** need 12+ closed loans to apply |
| "Get paid per unit" | paid on the combined production of that month's hires | paid a fixed amount for each loan |
| "Up to $30,000 per month" | the top of a one-time bonus scale | a $30,000 monthly salary |

Two of those three make the job sound like something the reader is not qualified for.

Now:

- The 12-loan rule is the largest type on the page: **"A hire only counts if *that loan officer*
  closed 12 or more loans in their last 12 months"** — followed by *"The 12 loans are theirs, not
  yours. You are not required to have closed anything."*
- "unit" is gone. It says "closed loan" everywhere.
- The bonus is one sentence of arithmetic — every 12 loans in the combined total is $1,000 to you,
  paid once, up to $30,000 — with a slider so you can see the figure for any total, and the full
  scale printed underneath it.
- The four performance targets keep their numbers and lose the jargon: 3 hires in the first 60 days /
  2 a month / 5–7 is a good month / 10 real conversations a week.

### Both pages

- A glossary at the bottom defines every internal word the page uses — ALLY, MMI, unit, good
  standing, qualified pipeline, KPI. None of them were defined before.
- "LO" is spelled out as "Loan Officer" everywhere.
