### How these were written

The old pages were drafted by AI from the two program documents you shared. These new ones are
drafted by AI as well — but from your feedback instead: *"Everyone should QUICKLY understand these
two programs."* Same programs, same numbers, rebuilt around how fast a stranger can understand them.

### The biggest change

Neither page used to admit the other one existed, so a loan officer had no way to tell whether they
were on the right page. Both now open with a two-card chooser — *"Pick the one that is you."*

**Ambassador.** The old headline, *"Recruit. Earn. Get Supported."*, never said who the page was for
or what you get — and the first number on the page was Level 1's marketing budget: **None**. Now the
headline is *"Know a good loan officer? Get paid to bring them here"*, and one line settles the
mental model: every loan officer who joins because of you pays you a referral bonus, and that is the
same at every level — the levels are only about how much marketing money we put behind you. The three
level cards, about forty bullets between them, became one four-row comparison table.

**Loan Officer Recruiter.** Three lines on the old page could be read backwards:

| Old line | What we meant | How it reads |
|---|---|---|
| "12+ verified units to qualify" | the person you recruit needs 12+ closed loans | **you** need 12+ closed loans to apply |
| "Get paid per unit" | paid on the combined production of that month's hires | paid a fixed amount for each loan |
| "Up to $30,000 per month" | the top of a one-time bonus scale | a $30,000 monthly salary |

Two of those three make the job sound like something the reader is not qualified for. So the 12-loan
rule is now the largest type on the page — *"A hire only counts if that loan officer closed 12 or
more loans in their last 12 months. The 12 loans are theirs, not yours."* — "unit" is replaced by
"closed loan" everywhere, and the bonus is one sentence of arithmetic with a slider, above the full
30-row scale from your document.

Both pages also got a glossary defining every internal word they use — ALLY, MMI, unit, good
standing, KPI — and "LO" is spelled out as "Loan Officer" throughout.
